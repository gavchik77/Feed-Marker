Why use a marker?

Marker principle

When feed intake or faecal output cannot be measured directly, an indigestible marker can be used. The marker becomes concentrated in faeces because digestible material disappears during digestion.

Internal markerNatural feed constituente.g. lignin, acid-insoluble ash, n-alkanes
External markerAdded to the feede.g. chromic oxide (Cr₂O₃)
Marker is assumedIndigestible
Main useEstimate faecal output
Faecal DM (kg/day) = marker dose (g/day) ÷ marker concentration in faeces (g/kg DM)
If only marker concentrations in feed and faeces are known
DM digestibility = (marker in faeces − marker in feed) ÷ marker in faeces

Example: marker rises from 10 g/kg DM in feed to 20 g/kg DM in faeces → (20 − 10) ÷ 20 = 0.50 = 50% DMD.

FindDM digestibility

Step A — estimate faecal DM output

Faecal DM = 10 g/day ÷ 4 g/kg DM

Step B — calculate digestibility

DMD (%) = [(DM intake − faecal DM output) ÷ DM intake] × 100

Compound digestibility coefficient = 1 − [(marker dose × compound in faeces) ÷ (marker in faeces × compound in feed × DM intake)]
A coefficient is written as a decimal (for example 0.67). To express it as a percentage, multiply by 100.

Formula summary

Three calculations to remember

1Faecal DM = dose ÷ faecal marker concentration
2DMD = (intake − faecal DM) ÷ intake
3Compound coefficient = 1 − marker-adjusted faecal fraction
CheckCoefficient × 100 = digestibility %
